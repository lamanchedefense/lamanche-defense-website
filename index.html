Quality

Every order follows the same five steps

The process is written down and applied the same way to a single part and to a full kit, so the record behind a delivery can always be produced.

1.

Source verification

Supplier, part number, drawing revision and domestic content requirements confirmed before anything is ordered.

Standards: DLAD Procurement Note C03 (traceability); DFARS 252.225-7001, and 252.225-7015 for hand and measuring tools.

2.

Receiving inspection

Incoming material checked against the purchase order and drawing, with nonconforming items held apart.

Standards: FAR 52.246-2; sampling per ANSI/ASQ Z1.4.

3.

Assembly to drawing

Kits and assemblies built to the drawing and contents list, with each item accounted for as it goes in.

Standards: drawing and TDP at the revision ordered; ASME Y14.5.

4.

Final inspection and records

Dimensional and visual checks completed, and the inspection and source records filed against the order.

Standards: FAR 4.703 record retention; calibration traceable to ISO/IEC 17025.

5.

Packing and labelling

Packed and labelled to MIL-STD-129, so the shipment arrives identified the way the receiving activity expects.

Standards: MIL-STD-2073 (packing); MIL-STD-129 (marking).

The name

Close enough to be brushed by the king's sleeve

The name was chosen from our own Scots and French ancestry, and from the one story in that shared history that carries both halves of it at once: Scots trusted at the heart of France.

La Manche is named for the Gardes de la Manche, the inner guard of the kings of France. From the Scottish Company of the royal bodyguard, the twenty-four oldest men were set apart as the Guards of the Sleeve. Their post was at the king's side, close enough to be brushed by his sleeve: in the hall, on campaign, and at his door through the night. Their captain was styled the First Man-at-arms of France.

They were defenders by station, not by occasion. The Scottish Company held its post without interruption for more than 360 years, a record without parallel in Europe, and its guards fell defending their king, at Montlhéry in 1465 and after. They were chosen for loyalty, trusted with defense, and kept for reliability, for four centuries.

That is the standard this name sets for us. Our work is to stand between our customers and failure: parts supplied to the drawing, kits assembled and inspected as though readiness depended on them, and quotes answered and deliveries made when promised. Loyalty, defense, reliability. It was a job description four centuries before it was ours.
